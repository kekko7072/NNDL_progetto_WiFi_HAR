\section{Concluding Remarks}
\label{sec:conclusions}

Starting from a baseline Wi-Fi HAR pipeline evaluated under a strict cross-domain
protocol ($48.2\%$ on unseen environments), we implemented and measured four
strategies one at a time: a supervised-contrastive representation ($+4.9$ points,
with a t-SNE confirming environment-invariance), a slower optimizer schedule
($+3.1$), an overlapping sliding window ($+11.1$) and multi-antenna soft decision
fusion ($+12.8$). The last two compose and take the \emph{same}
$128{,}535$-parameter network to $\mathbf{72.1\%}$, above the single-component
ablation of the reference system~\cite{sharp} on every unseen set.

Two of them cost almost nothing at training time---a stride constant and a
probability average over receiver diversity the dataset already contains---which
makes them the first things to try in a new deployment. The contrastive encoder
is the conditional case: it helps when labelled windows are scarce and stops
helping once the front-end supplies ten times more, so we read it as a
data-efficiency mechanism rather than a substitute for a good front-end. Still
missing: the strongest-path sanitization behind most of the remaining gap,
multi-seed runs to put error bars on differences of a few points, and fusion on
top of the frozen contrastive probe.

\paragraph{What we learned and difficulties encountered}
The strict environment-based split changes the story: modest-looking numbers are
the honest cost of never seeing the test environment, which a random split would
hide. Three results were counter-intuitive---training \emph{less} well
generalized \emph{better}; fine-tuning the contrastive encoder \emph{hurt}; and
the largest gains came from the two cheapest changes, once the pipeline carried a
group identifier. The main difficulties were getting SupCon pre-training to
converge (the loss barely moved), the run-to-run variance of a small model
trained on one environment, and the memory cost of overlapping windows, which
forced a two-pass, pre-allocating loader before the $91\%$-overlap front-end
could run at all.
