\section{Concluding Remarks}
\label{sec:conclusions}

Starting from a baseline Wi-Fi HAR pipeline under a strict cross-domain protocol
($48.2\%$ on unseen environments), we implemented and measured four strategies
one at a time: a supervised-contrastive representation ($+4.9$), a slower
optimizer schedule ($+3.1$), an overlapping sliding window ($+11.9$) and
multi-antenna soft decision fusion ($+12.6$). The last two compose and take
the \emph{same} $128{,}535$-parameter
network to $\mathbf{72.7\%}$, above the single-component ablation of the
reference system~\cite{sharp} on every unseen set, at almost no training
cost---a stride constant and a probability average over receiver diversity the
dataset already contains. The contrastive encoder is conditional: a
data-efficiency mechanism, not a front-end substitute. Still missing: the
strongest-path sanitization behind most of the remaining gap, multi-seed runs,
and fusion on top of the frozen probe with the contrastive stage re-tuned at
$10^{-3}$.

\paragraph{What we learned and difficulties encountered}
The strict environment-based split explains the modest numbers: never seeing
the test environment is an honest cost a random split would hide. Three
results were counter-intuitive---training \emph{less} well generalized
\emph{better}, fine-tuning the contrastive encoder \emph{hurt}, and the
largest gains came from the two cheapest changes once the pipeline carried a
group identifier. The main difficulties were getting SupCon pre-training to
converge (the loss barely moved), run-to-run variance on one environment, and
the memory cost of overlapping windows, which forced a pre-allocating loader
before the $91\%$-overlap front-end could run.
