\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

\textbf{Wi-Fi sensing and the SHARP system.}
Device-free HAR from commodity Wi-Fi has matured rapidly thanks to CSI extraction
tools and public datasets. The most relevant work for us is SHARP~\cite{sharp},
which recognizes activities from the Doppler information in IEEE~802.11ac CSI and
targets environment and person independence through two ingredients: (i) a robust phase-sanitization step that isolates the
multipath components and adopts the strongest path as phase reference, and
(ii) a decision-fusion scheme that runs a compact Inception-style CNN on
each of the four receiver antennas and combines their decisions. On the seven
measurement sets of the accompanying dataset~\cite{csidataset} the full system
exceeds $95\%$ even on rooms, days and persons never seen at training time. Those
results rest on a heavy, hand-engineered front-end: the paper's own ablation
shows that replacing the strongest-path sanitization with the standard
reference-antenna method makes the same CNN collapse to $39$--$60\%$ on
the unseen sets, i.e.\ much of the robustness is carried by the pre-processing
rather than the learned representation. Our work keeps the minimal front-end
and asks how much cross-domain robustness can be recovered elsewhere: on the
representation side (contrastive pre-training) and on the
decision side, by re-implementing SHARP's antenna fusion, which,
unlike sanitization, costs nothing at training time and turns out to be worth
more than any other change we made.

\textbf{Contrastive representation learning.}
Contrastive methods learn representations by pulling together ``views'' that
should be similar and pushing apart those that should differ.
SimCLR~\cite{simclr} established the self-supervised recipe of contrasting two
augmented views against in-batch negatives with a temperature-scaled InfoNCE
loss on $L_2$-normalized embeddings. Supervised Contrastive Learning
(SupCon)~\cite{supcon} extends it to
the labelled setting (all samples sharing the anchor's label become
positives) producing tighter, more class-consistent clusters than cross-entropy.
We adopt SupCon because its notion of ``positive'' maps onto our goal: two
windows of the same activity should be neighbours whatever environment,
person or antenna they come from. Combining it with explicit
domain-randomization augmentations for cross-domain Wi-Fi HAR on the SHARP data
is the extension we contribute, and \secref{sec:results} quantifies both its
gains and the regime in which they vanish.
