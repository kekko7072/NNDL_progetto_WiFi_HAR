\begin{abstract}
We study device-free human activity recognition (HAR) from the Doppler
spectrograms of Wi-Fi channel-state information (CSI), using the publicly
released traces of the SHARP dataset~\cite{sharp,csidataset}. Starting from the
Inception-style convolutional baseline of the course material---trained
\emph{only} on measurement set~$S1$ and evaluated on the six unseen sets
$S2$--$S7$, where it reaches $48.2\%$---we add and measure, one at a time, three
strategies for closing the cross-domain gap without the hand-engineered front-end
of the reference system. (i) \emph{Supervised contrastive pre-training}: a
batch-normalized encoder trained with a SupCon objective and domain-randomization
augmentations, with a linear probe on the frozen representation, gives $53.1\%$
and improves \emph{every} measurement set, a t-SNE analysis confirming that the
learned space is environment-invariant. (ii) \emph{A slower optimizer schedule}
reaches $51.3\%$ while fitting the training environment \emph{worse}, showing
that on a single-environment training set the two objectives conflict. (iii)
\emph{Multi-antenna soft decision fusion}: a recording-level group identifier
threaded through the data pipeline lets the four per-antenna probability vectors
of one physical time window be averaged, lifting accuracy to $\mathbf{61.5\%}$
($+10.2$ points, $+7.4$ to $+17.4$ per set) with no retraining and no extra
parameters---the largest gain we measured, enough to overtake the
single-component ablation of the reference system. We also report the front-end
work that makes an overlapping ($91\%$) sliding window affordable in memory. The
residual gap to the full pipeline ($>95\%$) is attributable to its strongest-path
phase sanitization, which we do not implement.
\end{abstract}
