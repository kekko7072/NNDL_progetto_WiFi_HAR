\begin{abstract}
We study device-free human activity recognition (HAR) from the Doppler
spectrograms of Wi-Fi channel-state information (CSI), using the publicly
released traces of the SHARP dataset~\cite{sharp,csidataset}. We first reproduce
the Inception-style convolutional baseline of the course material (developed by
M.~Carraro): trained \emph{only} on measurement set~$S1$ and evaluated on the
six unseen sets $S2$--$S7$, it reaches $48.2\%$ accuracy on the unseen domains,
quantifying how hard cross-domain generalization is when no domain-invariance
mechanism is used. We then add a Supervised Contrastive Learning (SupCon)
extension (developed by F.~Vezzani): a deeper, batch-normalized encoder is
pre-trained with a supervised-contrastive objective and domain-randomization
augmentations, and a linear probe is fit on the frozen representation. This
raises accuracy to $53.1\%$, improving over the baseline on \emph{every}
measurement set ($+4.9$ points overall), and a t-SNE analysis confirms the
learned space is environment-invariant. Both models remain well below the full
SHARP pipeline ($>95\%$ on every set), whose strongest-path phase sanitization
and multi-antenna \emph{decision fusion} we do not implement; we analyse this
gap and argue that the two ideas are complementary.
\end{abstract}
