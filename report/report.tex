\begin{abstract}
We study device-free human activity recognition (HAR) from the Doppler
spectrograms of Wi-Fi channel-state information (CSI), using the publicly
released traces of the SHARP dataset~\cite{sharp,csidataset}. Starting from the
Inception-style convolutional baseline of the course material---trained
\emph{only} on measurement set~$S1$ and evaluated on the six unseen sets
$S2$--$S7$, where it reaches $48.2\%$---we add and measure, one change at a time,
four strategies for closing the cross-domain gap without the hand-engineered
front-end of the reference system. (i) \emph{Supervised contrastive
pre-training}: a batch-normalized encoder trained with a SupCon objective and
domain-randomization augmentations, with a linear probe on the frozen
representation, gives $53.1\%$ and improves \emph{every} measurement set. (ii) \emph{A
slower optimizer schedule} reaches $51.3\%$ while fitting the training environment
\emph{worse}, showing that on a single-environment training set the two
objectives conflict. (iii) \emph{An overlapping sliding window} at the stride used
by the dataset authors ($30$ samples, $91\%$ overlap, $\approx\!10\times$ more
windows), made affordable by a two-pass, memory-bounded loader, lifts the same
baseline to $60.1\%$. (iv) \emph{Multi-antenna soft decision fusion}: a
recording-level group identifier threaded through the data pipeline lets the four
per-antenna probability vectors of one physical time window be averaged, adding
$+12.6$ points with no retraining and no extra parameters. Together the last two
reach $\mathbf{72.7\%}$ ($+24.5$ over the initial baseline) and beat the
single-component ablation of the reference system on every unseen set. Notably,
contrastive pre-training helps in the data-poor regime but not once the sliding
window supplies ten times more windows. The residual gap to the full pipeline
($>95\%$) is attributable to its strongest-path phase sanitization, which we do
not implement.
\end{abstract}
