\subsection{Baseline}
On the pooled unseen test set the baseline reaches $\mathbf{48.2\%}$ (test loss
$1.052$), from $45.5\%$ ($S2$) to $52.3\%$ ($S7$). Its confusion matrix
(\fig{fig:cm}\,(a)) has two dominant error modes: \emph{sitting still} is
absorbed by \emph{empty} ($L\!\rightarrow\!E$: $0.76$), both near-static and
concentrated at zero Doppler velocity, and \emph{running}/\emph{walking} are
heavily confused ($0.39$ and $0.28$).

\subsection{Contrastive representation}
The linear probe on the frozen SupCon encoder raises accuracy to
$\mathbf{53.1\%}$ (validation $58.3\%$): $+4.9$ points and an improvement on
\emph{every} set, the largest on $S3$ ($+8.3$, unseen person) and $S7$ ($+5.9$,
all new). Its confusion matrix (\fig{fig:cm}\,(b)) sharpens \emph{running} and
softens $L\!\rightarrow\!E$ ($0.76\!\rightarrow\!0.52$). \emph{End-to-end
fine-tuning} then \emph{hurts}, dropping to $48.4\%$: freezing the pre-trained
encoder is what generalizes, fine-tuning re-overfits the one environment.

The t-SNE projection (\fig{fig:tsne}, from the sliding-window run of
\secref{sec:sliding}) is the most informative diagnostic. Colored by
\emph{environment} (right) the points are thoroughly mixed---the
SupCon objective \emph{did} reach its design goal of an environment-invariant
representation. Colored by \emph{activity} (left) the space splits into a
static region (\emph{empty}, \emph{sitting}) and a moving arc (\emph{jumping},
\emph{running}, \emph{walking}), but the activities \emph{within} each region
stay partly entangled: contrastive learning removed the \emph{domain} nuisance
and exposed a harder, intrinsic limit---a single-antenna $\approx\!2$\,s window
does not fully separate the activities, whichever way it is encoded.
Consistently, in both runs the SupCon loss fell only from $\approx\!4.6$ to
$\approx\!4.3$: the pre-training never fully converged.

\subsection{Optimizer schedule}
The slow schedule changes only the learning rate, the patience and the epoch
budget, so the two runs isolate that factor. Slowing
training down \emph{improves} cross-domain accuracy from $48.2\%$ to $51.3\%$
($+3.1$ points, five sets out of six) even though the model fits its own training
data \emph{worse} ($0.575$ vs.\ $0.601$, \fig{fig:curves}). With a single
training environment the two objectives conflict: an epoch spent fitting $S1$
better is also an epoch spent memorizing its room, person and day. It also
tempers the previous subsection---$+3.1$ of the contrastive model's $+4.9$ points
can be bought with an optimizer setting alone, so single-run gains of a few
points here are fragile.

\subsection{Sliding-window front-end}
\label{sec:sliding}
Cutting the same recordings at stride $30$ instead of $340$ turns $13{,}564$
windows into $152{,}320$ and takes the \emph{unchanged} architecture from
$48.2\%$ to $\mathbf{59.3\%}$ ($+11.1$ points, $+8.4$ to $+15.3$ per set). Two
caveats keep this honest: the run also raises the early-stopping patience from
$7$ to $12$, so part of the gain belongs to the longer schedule, and the extra
windows are strongly correlated, so the effective sample size grows far less than
tenfold. The cost is real---$\approx\!21$\,GB resident and an epoch an order of
magnitude slower, exactly why the loader had to allocate once instead of
concatenating---but \fig{fig:curves}(c) shows the denser windows buy genuine
fitting capacity, not only noise: training accuracy reaches $0.756$ with
validation accuracy $12$ points above the non-overlapping run.

\subsection{Multi-antenna decision fusion}
Averaging the four per-antenna probability vectors (\eq{eq:fusion}) is the
largest single improvement we measured, and it is free: no retraining, no extra
parameters, one softmax and one group-average at inference. On the
sliding-window model it lifts $59.3\%$ to $\mathbf{72.1\%}$ ($+12.8$ points, from
$+6.9$ on $S7$ to $+17.1$ on $S2$); on the non-overlapping model it gave $+10.2$
($51.3\!\rightarrow\!61.5\%$). The gain is therefore robust to the data regime.

Its mechanism changes with that regime. At stride $340$ fusion sharpened the
motion classes but left \emph{sitting}$\rightarrow$\emph{empty} untouched: with
almost no motion in a window the four antennas agreed on the same wrong answer.
At stride $30$ the single-antenna model is already half-right on that pair
(\fig{fig:cm}\,(c), $L$ at $0.50$) and fusion turns it into the largest per-class
gain---$L$ reaches $0.75$, $L\!\rightarrow\!E$ falls from $0.45$ to $0.23$---next
to \emph{jumping} ($0.56\!\rightarrow\!0.72$) and \emph{running}
($0.63\!\rightarrow\!0.75$). Averaging only helps where the antennas disagree,
and denser coverage is what creates that disagreement on low-motion windows.

\subsection{Does contrastive learning still help?}
Repeating the contrastive stage on the sliding-window data answers a question the
first experiment could not: the linear probe now reaches $58.3\%$, $1.0$ point
\emph{below} the plain baseline it had beaten by $4.9$, and fine-tuning again
does worse ($57.0\%$). The contrastive stage thus behaves as a
\emph{data-efficiency} mechanism more than a domain-invariance one: with $2767$
training windows it buys a representation cross-entropy cannot learn, with
$31{,}103$ (correlated) windows cross-entropy catches up by itself. One confound:
the contrastive and probe learning rates were left at $10^{-4}$ in that run while
the baseline used $10^{-3}$, so part of the deficit may be an under-trained
encoder rather than the data regime.

\subsection{Comparison with the SHARP paper}
\tab{tab:compare} places our results next to the SHARP paper~\cite{sharp}: the
\emph{full} system (strongest-path sanitization $+$ 4-antenna fusion) and an
\emph{ablation} that swaps the sanitization for the standard reference-antenna
method, feeding the \emph{same} CNN (\cite{sharp}, Table~4). Full SHARP stays
above $95\%$ everywhere. Our best configuration (sliding window $+$ fusion,
$72.1\%$) beats the ablation on \emph{every} unseen set, by $10.6$ ($S7$) to
$26.8$ points ($S5$), with the largest margins exactly where the ablation
collapses---the blocked-line-of-sight sets $S4$ and $S5$. The residual
$\sim\!26$-point gap to full SHARP is thus concentrated in the one ingredient we
do not implement. The acquisition campaigns behind the dataset also spanned
nearly two years (\cite{sharp}, Sec.~5.2), and that time diversity is part of
what the reference results build on.

\subsection{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What each change bought}
Ranked by measured effect, decision fusion ($+12.8$) and the sliding window
($+11.1$) dominate the contrastive representation ($+4.9$, and $-1.0$ once data
is plentiful) and the optimizer schedule ($+3.1$). The ordering is a finding in
itself: the two cheapest changes---a stride constant and a few lines of
post-processing over information already in the dataset---beat the one that cost
the most design effort, and they compose, taking the \emph{same}
$128{,}535$-parameter network from $48.2\%$ to $72.1\%$.

\paragraph{Why we are still far from SHARP}
The gap is now essentially one component. SHARP uses the \emph{strongest}
multipath component as phase reference, and its ablation shows the standard
reference-antenna method makes the \emph{same} CNN collapse to $39$--$60\%$
(\tab{tab:compare}, last column). Our best model sits $10$ to $27$ points
\emph{above} that ablation: we reproduced the decision-level half of SHARP's
robustness and improved on the data side, but none of its signal-processing half.
Training on one small environment also makes every number high-variance.

\paragraph{Where representation learning fits}
The SupCon stage attacks domain shift on the \emph{representation} axis and, as
the t-SNE shows, succeeds there---but its measured advantage evaporates once the
front-end supplies ten times more windows. Contrastive pre-training is therefore
worth its complexity when labelled windows are scarce, the realistic setting for
a deployment with one calibration environment, while in the data-rich regime the
effort is better spent on the front-end and the decision rule. The experiment
left undone is fusion on top of the frozen probe with the contrastive stage
re-tuned at $10^{-3}$: every component exists in the released code, but no run
combines them.
