\subsection{Baseline and optimizer schedule}
On the pooled unseen test set the baseline reaches $\mathbf{48.2\%}$ (test loss
$1.052$), from $45.5\%$ ($S2$) to $52.3\%$ ($S7$). Its confusion matrix
(\fig{fig:cm}\,(a)) has two dominant error modes: sitting still is
absorbed by empty ($L\!\rightarrow\!E$: $0.76$), both near-static and
concentrated at zero Doppler velocity, and running/walking are
heavily confused ($0.39$ and $0.28$).

The slow schedule improves accuracy to $51.3\%$ ($+3.1$ points) even though
training accuracy is lower ($0.575$ instead of $0.601$). With only one training
environment, fitting the training set better can also mean learning more
environment-specific details.

\subsection{Contrastive representation}
The linear probe on the frozen SupCon encoder raises accuracy to
$\mathbf{53.1\%}$ (validation $58.3\%$): $+4.9$ points and an improvement on
every set, the largest on $S3$ ($+8.3$, unseen person) and $S7$ ($+5.9$).
Its confusion matrix (\fig{fig:cm}\,(b)) sharpens running and softens
$L\!\rightarrow\!E$ ($0.76\!\rightarrow\!0.52$). Fine-tuning then
hurts, dropping to $48.4\%$: freezing the encoder is what generalizes instead
fine-tuning re-overfits the one environment.

The t-SNE projection (\fig{fig:tsne}, from the sliding-window run of
\secref{sec:sliding}) is the most informative diagnostic. Colored by
environment (right) the points are totally mixed; the
SupCon objective did reach its design goal of an environment-invariant
representation. Colored by activity (left) the space splits into a
static region (empty, sitting) and a moving arc (jumping,
running, walking), but the activities within each region
stay partly entangled: contrastive learning removed the domain nuisance
and exposed a harder, intrinsic limit: a single-antenna $\approx\!2$\,s window
does not fully separate the activities, whichever way it is encoded.
Consistently, in both runs the SupCon loss fell only from $\approx\!4.5$ to
$\approx\!4.3$: the pre-training never fully converged.

With sliding-window data, the SupCon probe reaches $59.5\%$, slightly below the
$60.1\%$ baseline, and fine-tuning reaches $58.0\%$. Thus, contrastive learning
helps when training data is limited, but its advantage disappears when many
more windows are available.

\subsection{Sliding-window front-end}
\label{sec:sliding}
Cutting the same recordings at stride $30$ instead of $340$ turns $13{,}564$
windows into $152{,}320$ and takes the unchanged architecture from
$48.2\%$ to $\mathbf{60.1\%}$ ($+11.9$ points, $+8.6$ to $+16.1$ per set). Two
caveats: the run also raises patience from $7$ to $12$, so part of the gain
belongs to the longer schedule, and the extra windows are correlated and the
effective sample size grows far less than tenfold. The cost is
$\approx\!21$\,GB resident and an epoch an order of magnitude slower but the denser windows
buy real fitting capacity, not noise: $0.750$ training accuracy with validation
$12$ points above the non-overlapping run.

\subsection{Multi-antenna decision fusion}
Averaging the four per-antenna probability vectors (\eq{eq:fusion}) is the
largest single improvement we measured, and it is free: no retraining, no extra
parameters, one softmax and one group-average at inference. On the
sliding-window model it lifts $60.1\%$ to $\mathbf{72.7\%}$ ($+12.6$ points, from
$+6.0$ on $S7$ to $+16.9$ on $S6$); on the non-overlapping model it gave $+10.2$
($51.3\!\rightarrow\!61.5\%$) so robust to the data regime.

Its mechanism changes with the regime. At stride $340$ fusion sharpened the
motion classes but left sitting$\rightarrow$empty untouched: with
almost no motion the four antennas agreed on the same wrong answer. At stride
$30$ the single-antenna model is already half-right on that pair
(\fig{fig:cm}\,(c), $L$ at $0.49$) and fusion turns it into the largest per-class
gain: $L$ reaches $0.71$, $L\!\rightarrow\!E$ falls from $0.46$ to $0.27$ next
to jumping ($0.57\!\rightarrow\!0.73$) and running
($0.65\!\rightarrow\!0.80$). Averaging helps only where antennas disagree, and
denser coverage is what creates that disagreement on low-motion windows.

\subsection{Comparison with the SHARP paper}
\tab{tab:compare} places our results next to SHARP~\cite{sharp}: the full
system (strongest-path sanitization $+$ 4-antenna fusion) and an ablation
that swaps the sanitization for the standard reference-antenna method, feeding
the same CNN (\cite{sharp}, Table~4). Full SHARP stays above $95\%$
everywhere. Our best configuration ($72.7\%$) beats the ablation on every
unseen set, by $10.4$ ($S7$) to $27.2$ points ($S5$), with the largest margins
exactly where it collapses, that is the blocked-line-of-sight sets. The residual
$\sim\!26$-point gap to full SHARP is thus concentrated in the one ingredient we
do not implement.
